Washington, Feb. 20.—The Presi-
dent, in answer to Senatorial questions
regarding violations of the civil rights
bill, says the Secretary of the Navy is
aware of none; Secretary of the Inte-
rior has received no information of
any; Secretary of State has none;
Postmaster-General has none; Attor-
ney-General has reported none. - The
case reported by William Firshee, of
Georgia, under consideration, referred
to Attorney-General’s office by the
Secretary of War. Secretary of War
says necessary orders were issued. No
neglect or refusal reported to the
President. Three cases reported to
President: Dr. Watson, Va.; Wm.
Firsher, Ga.; Parkins, Texas.
In response to Senate enquiry, Stan-
ton reported three cases involving a
lame execution of the civil rights bill,
including Dr. Watson, for killing a
negro. None others came to the know-
ledge of the War Department or Presi-
dent within Stanton’s knowledge.
Cowan made a defence of the Presi-
dent and the South, based on Stanton’s
report, and asked why Senators go to
Secretary Stanton for redress of al-
leged outrages; instead of pouring
complaints into the public ear, respect
for the country’s fair fame should in-
duce them to seek redress through the
willing Stanton, before proclaiming
them as a means of maligning the
President.
Sherman, urging his bill, said we
give to negroes the ballot and school
protection laws, but the nation never
would consent to put white men under
their domination. He designated Ste-
vens and Elliott’s plan as horribly de-
fective.
